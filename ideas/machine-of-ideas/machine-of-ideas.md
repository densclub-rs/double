# Idea: Machine of Ideas

![Machine of Ideas](../../_media/machine-of-ideas/machine-of-ideas-illustration.png)


## 1. Summary

"Machine of Ideas" is the result of many years of reflection on the problems of computer science and, more specifically, on the problems of programming. Here, programming is understood primarily not as writing code, but as the work of understanding an idea, designing a system, clarifying interactions, data formats, and ways of working with data, after which direct implementation begins.

At its core, the machine of ideas is an attempt to formalize individual experience in working with ideas, knowledge, and system design into a reproducible process that can turn raw thoughts, observations, and conversations into structured artifacts. As a further development of this idea, the machine may become a way to work not only with one person's experience, but also with the experience of different people, making the formation of collective experience possible.

This idea is not conceived as something entirely invented from scratch. It resonates with lines of thought already developed in science and psychology, especially around the externalization of unfinished thought, the dynamics of tension and completion, and the organization of action through external cognitive supports. In that sense, it stands close to the work of [Bluma Zeigarnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluma_Zeigarnik), [Kurt Lewin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Lewin), and, most explicitly, [David Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Allen_%28author%29).

Its foundations are more philosophical than pragmatic: it declares the most general principles of working with ideas and knowledge without binding them to a specific implementation or representation format. The main philosophical basis of the machine of ideas is epistemology and the foundations of scientific knowledge, while one of its strongest practical resonances is Allen's idea of an "external brain" and the [GTD (Getting Things Done) method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getting_Things_Done).

At the same time, the machine of ideas is not a demand for strict adherence to any one school or methodology. It treats such approaches as useful foundations that can be individually adapted. Within Double, the emphasis remains on the subject: on subjective knowledge, subjective experience, and a way of working that fits a particular person rather than enforcing a universal behavioral template.

The machine should also support multilingual work as a first-class part of that subjective fit. A person may think, speak, and clarify an idea in the language that is natural for them, while the final artifacts may be produced in the same language or in another language chosen for publication, collaboration, research, or later implementation work. Therefore agents should explicitly offer a choice of language for the dialogue and a separate choice of language for the final artifacts before generating idea, concept, or principle artifacts.

## 2. Context / Origin

- The idea arose in the context of the Double project, where knowledge and ideas need to accumulate in an open, understandable, and continuously developing form.
- It also emerged from the recognition that many of its core intuitions already have serious scientific and methodological precedents, especially in work concerned with unfinished cognitive material, action-oriented structuring, and external supports for thinking.
- Its likely implementation is conceived as a set of agents, roles, and modes that are activated at specific stages of an idea-management flow.
- This flow is needed to make long-term and parallel work on many ideas possible, while preserving the result of each iteration in a form that remains understandable to both humans and AI.
- Markdown is chosen as that form because it is simple, readable, and suitable both for structured work and for long-term accumulation of idea artifacts.
- The artifact language should be explicit: the user may communicate in one language while asking the machine to produce the final Markdown artifact in another.
- David Allen's [GTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getting_Things_Done) is especially relevant here because it demonstrates a practical way to move mental tension into a trusted external system. The machine of ideas extends this intuition beyond task management toward the preservation and development of ideas themselves.
- The same format also makes it easy to build a digital garden of ideas and publish them on the internet, whether as a standalone website, as blog-like materials, or as public artifacts that can attract attention and discussion.
- In this sense, the machine of ideas is intended to make ideas more alive: each iteration of work on an idea can become immediately available for study, revision, and further development, which is especially useful for collaboration in small groups of like-minded people.
- Unlike a canonical GTD-style system, however, Double does not treat the workflow as fixed doctrine. The expectation is that each person may reshape the machine around their own habits, modes of thought, and experience while preserving continuity and traceability.

## 3. Problem / Motivation

- There are already exist rules for describing ideas (in [Ideas](../ideas.md)), but there is still no explicitly formulated machine that can systematically lead an idea through stages of clarification, development, and formalization.
- Without such a flow, raw thoughts easily lose their context, prematurely solidify into architecture, or remain fragmented notes that are difficult to revisit and develop further.
- This problem is not merely technical. It is related to a broader human difficulty already studied in psychology and behavior-oriented approaches: unresolved material continues to occupy attention until it is given an adequate external form.
- The machine of ideas responds to that problem by treating writing, clarification, and structuring not just as documentation, but as cognitive relief and as a way to preserve subjective meaning outside the mind.
- This matters because Double is concerned not only with storing ideas, but with accumulating, sharing, and developing knowledge in a form that can remain alive over time.
- The goal, however, is not to copy scientific or methodological frameworks literally. It is to adapt their strongest insights into a personal knowledge practice centered on the concrete subject who thinks, remembers, forgets, and develops ideas in their own way.

## 4. Raw Description

The machine of ideas is conceived as a general process for turning living, unfinished thought into forms that can be preserved, examined, developed, and shared. It begins not with a specification, architecture, or task, but with free expression: a person speaks or writes an idea in the form in which it currently exists, with its ambiguities, associations, gaps, intuitions, and unresolved tensions.

The first movement of the machine is preservation of this living context. An idea should not be forced too quickly into the language of implementation. At the beginning, it is often more important to retain the source movement of thought than to make it neat. The machine should help distinguish what is already clear from what is only emerging, what belongs to the idea itself from what is merely a possible future form, and what must remain open until later stages.

In this sense, the machine shares a family resemblance with approaches that take unfinished cognitive material seriously. [Zeigarnik's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluma_Zeigarnik) work makes unfinishedness itself visible as a force, [Lewin's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Lewin) field-oriented view helps frame thought and action as situated and tension-bearing, and [Allen's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Allen_%28author%29) externalization practices show how a trusted outside system can carry part of the burden of thinking. The machine of ideas borrows from this lineage mainly at the level of principles rather than by reproducing a fixed method.

From there, the idea can gradually be shaped into artifacts. These artifacts are not final answers, but stable traces of work: they make it possible to return to the idea, compare versions of understanding, extract concepts, synthesize principles, and prepare later forms of design without losing the original source. Each artifact should clarify a particular layer of the idea while preserving visible continuity with the material from which it came.

The machine therefore has to work as a sequence of transformations rather than as a single act of writing. Raw material becomes an idea artifact; an idea artifact can become a source for concepts; concepts can become a source for principles; principles can later guide proposals, specifications, designs, or other forms of action. The important point is not the fixed naming of these stages, but the preservation of meaningful transitions between them.

At the same time, the machine of ideas should remain capable of changing its own forms. Work with ideas always reveals new needs: some ideas require more questioning, some require conceptual mapping, some require the separation of conflicting assumptions, and some require a pause before formalization. A good machine of ideas should not impose one rigid template on all thought, but should be able to refine its stages, artifacts, and questions while preserving traceability to the original idea.

This adaptability is essential. The point is not obedience to neuro-behaviorist or productivity doctrine, but the construction of an external thinking environment that is personally meaningful and practically useful. Double therefore gives priority to subjective fit over methodological purity.

## 5. Key Elements

- raw user input
- clarification dialog
- preservation of the original thought context
- recognition that the idea builds on already explored scientific and psychological foundations rather than emerging in isolation
- resonance with Zeigarnik, Lewin, and especially David Allen's "external brain" orientation
- adaptation of established methods to the individual subject instead of strict adherence to a fixed doctrine
- structured idea artifacts
- gradual transformation from raw material to more formal layers
- traceability between source material and derived artifacts
- distinction between the idea itself and possible future implementations
- subsequent idea development stages
- the machine’s ability to change its own working forms through user interaction
- emphasis on subjective knowledge and subjective experience as first-class material
- multilingual dialogue and artifact generation as a first-class support for subjective fit, collaboration, and publication
- explicit separation between the language of interaction and the language of generated artifacts
- Markdown templates and artifact organization
- an explicit distinction between the idea-description level and the implementation level built from this conceptual description

## 6. Assumptions

- Markdown is suitable as the base format for storing the idea and related artifacts.
- The language of the final artifact should be selected by the user and recorded in artifact metadata.
- The language of conversation may differ from the language of the final artifact.
- An idea should first be preserved and clarified in the form closest to its original expression before it is translated into more formal layers.
- A repeatable process is needed for working with ideas, not just a collection of one-off notes.
- This process should not be rigidly fixed forever; it should be able to evolve as work with ideas reveals new needs.
- The same general process may require different forms of questioning, structuring, and artifact creation for different kinds of ideas.
- The description of the machine of ideas should remain distinct from any particular implementation of the machine.
- A future implementation should be traceable back to the conceptual description from which it was derived.

## 7. Examples / Scenarios

- A person formulates an idea in free form. The machine of ideas helps preserve the original context, asks clarifying questions, and gradually turns the material into a stable written form.
- A person discusses an idea in Russian, Serbian, Spanish, or another native language, but chooses English for the final artifact so it can be shared with collaborators or used as input for later technical work.
- A group discusses the same idea in one shared working language while producing concept and principle artifacts in another language for publication.
- A raw thought becomes a source for later work: first it is clarified as an idea, then its main concepts can be extracted, and later its guiding principles can be synthesized.
- A group of like-minded people returns to an already developed idea and can continue from a coherent artifact instead of reconstructing meaning from fragmented notes or past conversations.
- An idea contains several unresolved tensions. The machine does not force an immediate decision, but records the tensions so they can become material for later conceptual or principled work.
- Over time, repeated work with ideas reveals that the current form of description is insufficient. The machine can then refine its questions, artifacts, or stages while preserving continuity with the original conceptual description.

## 8. Signals of Value

- The idea reduces the risk of losing context at the earliest stage of working with new thoughts.
- It lets people work in the language where their thought is alive without forcing all final artifacts into that same language.
- It makes knowledge more comparable and suitable for collective development within Double.
- It creates a visible path from raw expression to more structured forms of understanding.
- It helps prevent premature movement from an unfinished idea to architecture, requirements, or implementation.
- If the machine of ideas works, it can become the entry point for a broader knowledge-development process.
- If the machine can meaningfully adapt its own working forms, it will better fit actual user practice rather than a preset scheme.
- A clear separation between conceptual description and future implementation reduces the risk of confusing the idea with one of its possible technical forms.

## 9. Resolved Questions

- The machine of ideas should begin with raw expression and move gradually toward more formal artifacts.
- The first stable transformation is the movement from an unfinished thought to a written idea that preserves enough context for later work.
- Later development can proceed through conceptual clarification, principle synthesis, and other forms of structured work without forcing immediate implementation.
- An idea is ready for further development when its source context, central concern, unresolved questions, and possible directions are visible enough to be revisited.
- The conceptual description of the machine should remain separate from the concrete directory or system in which it may later be implemented.
- Changes to the machine's forms, questions, stages, or artifacts count as evolution of the machine when they preserve continuity with the original idea and remain traceable to its conceptual description.
- Language selection is part of artifact formation: agents should ask which language to use for communication and which language to use for final artifacts, and artifact metadata should record the selected artifact language.
